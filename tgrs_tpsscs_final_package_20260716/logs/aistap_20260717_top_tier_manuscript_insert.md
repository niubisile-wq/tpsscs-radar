# AISTAP 20260717 Top-Tier Manuscript Insert

Date: 2026-07-17

## One-Sentence Argument

In low-false-alarm radar detection, compact TP-SSCS improves official AISTAP-SIM full-asset target detection by preserving target support under empirical false-alarm calibration, remains stable across training seeds and strengthened CFAR/logistic baselines, shows broad frame-level, paired-significance, whole-surface AUC, and component-attribution support, preserves a label-cost Pareto advantage over low-label HGB under severe positive-target-pixel scarcity, has a bounded local CPU runtime advantage over the checked raw/residual HGB inference stack, and provides useful features for a stronger supervised HGB detector, while the strongest raw/residual HGB result is retained as a supervised in-domain upper boundary and external/runtime claims remain bounded.

## Results Insert

Two additional full-asset audits strengthened the official AISTAP-SIM claim. First, the fixed detector was re-evaluated across three trainable-gate seeds (`7`, `11`, and `23`) on `simMed_test.mat` and `simWind_test.mat`. All three seeds preserved the same official full-asset win pattern, giving 21/21 combined wins against raw maps, 21/21 combined wins against `low_rank_residual_k30`, 42/42 asset-level wins against raw maps, and 42/42 asset-level wins against the low-rank residual comparator. Across the evaluated Pfa grid, the maximum cross-seed target-Pd range was only `0.0079`.

Second, we expanded the in-domain classical baseline family beyond raw and low-rank global top-k scores, and then allowed the local CFAR baselines to compete over multiple parameter settings. Raw and rank-matched residual maps were converted into CA-, GOCA-, SOCA-, and OS-CFAR local score maps, with training cells `4,6,8`, guard cells `1,2`, and OS percentiles `60,75,90`. TP-SSCS beat the best swept classical method at every combined operating point and every asset-level comparison, with 7/7 combined wins and 14/14 asset-level wins over the best of 75 global/local CFAR method/configuration candidates. The minimum combined TP-SSCS margin over this stronger swept baseline family remained positive at `0.0162`.

Third, we added a leave-one-condition-out learned baseline on the official AISTAP-SIM full assets. A supervised logistic detector was trained on raw-score local features from one official full-test condition and evaluated on the other condition, then the train/test direction was swapped. This learned comparator used no TP-SSCS residuals and no target coordinates, and it was thresholded by the same empirical-Pfa rule. TP-SSCS beat the LOSO learned baseline with 7/7 combined wins and 14/14 asset-level wins over 210 held-out target-bearing items. The minimum combined margin was `0.0596`, and paired bootstrap CI lower bounds were positive at all seven operating points.

Fourth, we ran a stronger nonlinear feature-ensemble boundary. A raw/residual HGB trained on one official full asset and tested on the other beat compact TP-SSCS at all seven combined Pfa points, so compact TP-SSCS should not be claimed as superior to all supervised learned detectors. Adding TP-SSCS gate and enhanced-score features to the same HGB family beat compact TP-SSCS at all seven combined points and nearly matched the raw/residual HGB boundary, with 6/7 combined wins versus raw/residual HGB and minimum combined delta `-0.0007`. This result is best treated as a claim-control and feature-value audit: TP-SSCS is useful as a compact detector and as a feature source, but strong supervised feature ensembles define an in-domain upper bound.

Fifth, we separated this supervised upper bound from target-instance label efficiency. A raw/residual HGB was trained with only a fixed number of positive target pixels from one official full asset and tested on the other, repeated over three seeds and both train/test directions. Compact TP-SSCS uses no positive target labels from these official full assets at this stage. It beat the low-label HGB at all seven combined Pfa points for `1`, `2`, `4`, and `8` positive-pixel budgets, and the bootstrap lower bounds for compact-minus-HGB Pd were positive at all seven Pfa points for those budgets. The same audit records the boundary: with `16` positive target pixels, the HGB first catches or exceeds compact TP-SSCS at an operating point.

Sixth, we converted that label-efficiency result into a label-cost Pareto audit by integrating each budget's seven-point curve into log-Pfa AUC and combining it with the local CPU runtime profile. Compact TP-SSCS has AUC `0.5313`, uses `0` official full-asset positive target labels, and runs at `133.66` ms/frame in the measured local profile. The low-label raw/residual HGB stack is `4.56x` slower in the same profile and remains AUC-dominated by compact TP-SSCS for budgets `1`, `2`, `4`, `8`, and `16` with positive bootstrap CIs. HGB first exceeds compact AUC at budget `64`, so the result is a resource-aware Pareto statement rather than a claim against the full-label HGB upper boundary.

Seventh, we audited whether the result depends on using target-bearing frame backgrounds for empirical-Pfa threshold selection. Thresholds estimated only from target-free frames preserve positive TP-SSCS margins over raw and low-rank baselines in both same-asset and cross-asset calibration modes, with 7/7 combined wins and positive bootstrap support in each mode. The fixed transferred thresholds are not fully empirical-Pfa calibrated on target-bearing backgrounds, so this result should be used as a threshold-source robustness and boundary audit rather than as a replacement for the main empirical-Pfa protocol.

Eighth, we audited whether the official mean Pd gains are broadly distributed across frames. Across `210` target-bearing frames and seven Pfa points, TP-SSCS has `1470/1470` nonnegative item-Pfa pairs versus `low_rank_residual_k30`, with no low-rank-favorable item-Pfa pairs. The raw comparison remains broad but not universal: the minimum combined win fraction is `0.890`, and `61` item-Pfa pairs favor raw maps. This result rules out a few-frame explanation versus the rank-matched residual while keeping the raw-map comparison properly bounded.

Ninth, we added a paired nonparametric significance audit over the same frozen frame-level rows. All `14` combined comparator/Pfa exact sign tests remain significant after BH-FDR correction, with worst combined q-value `2.945e-29` and minimum matched sign effect `0.816`. The sign test ignores ties, so this result should be reported as frame-level statistical support rather than as pixel-level independence or universal raw-frame dominance.

Tenth, we integrated each frame's seven-point operating curve into a normalized log-Pfa AUC over the checked `1e-5` to `1e-2` grid. TP-SSCS reaches mean AUC `0.5313`, compared with `0.4760` for `low_rank_residual_k30` and `0.3085` for raw maps. The minimum combined AUC delta is `0.0553`, the minimum frame-bootstrap CI lower bound is `0.0491`, and the worst combined BH-FDR q-value is `1.464e-55`. This supports whole-operating-surface robustness within the checked Pfa grid, not extrapolation beyond it.

Eleventh, we added a component-attribution audit over the same frozen detector-candidate rows. The finished detector improves log-Pfa AUC by `+0.2228` over raw and `+0.0553` over `low_rank_residual_k30`, with `195/15/0` frame-level AUC wins/ties/losses versus low-rank. The gate-only endpoint is deliberately reported as a boundary: its mean AUC is nearly tied with the finished detector and it is stronger at looser Pfa points, while the finished detector is stronger at the tightest Pfa. The selected policy should therefore be presented as a conservative low-false-alarm detector, not as a universal Pd upper bound.

Twelfth, we profiled inference cost on the local CPU to bound the deployment-cost story. Over 12 deterministic target-bearing official full-asset frames, compact TP-SSCS finished-detector inference had median runtime `133.66` ms/frame, while the checked raw/residual HGB inference stack had median runtime `608.99` ms/frame (`4.56x` HGB/compact ratio). The TP-SSCS gate has `2641` trainable parameters. This audit should be reported as a local runtime/complexity boundary, not as a hardware-independent real-time claim.

## Methods Insert

The strengthened classical-baseline audit used the same target-bearing official full-asset frames and the same target Pfa grid as the main AISTAP-SIM protocol. In addition to raw power and `low_rank_residual_k30` global top-k scores, the audit constructed local CA-CFAR, GOCA-CFAR, SOCA-CFAR, and OS-CFAR score maps on both raw and low-rank residual power maps. The parameter sweep varied training-window size, guard-window size, and OS percentile, then selected the best classical comparator separately at each asset/Pfa point. Local CFAR scores were used as normalized detector score maps rather than as analytically thresholded detectors; the final threshold for every score family was selected from target-free background pixels using the same conservative `score > threshold` empirical-Pfa rule. This design keeps the comparison focused on target separability under a shared false-alarm budget.

The full-asset seed-sensitivity audit retrained the compact TP-SSCS gate at seeds `7`, `11`, and `23` with the same rank, hidden width, training-step count, and learning rate. Each saved state was evaluated on the two official AISTAP-SIM full-test assets using the same finished-detector policy and seven Pfa operating points. The audit reports combined wins, asset-level wins, and cross-seed target-Pd ranges rather than treating any single seed as the sole evidence for the finished detector.

The LOSO learned-baseline audit fits a raw-feature logistic detector using frame-normalized log raw power, local z-scores, local contrasts, and gradient magnitude. One direction trains on `simMed_test.mat` and tests on `simWind_test.mat`; the other direction trains on `simWind_test.mat` and tests on `simMed_test.mat`. Each direction uses 128 training frames, 105 positive training frames, 3943 positive pixels, and 262144 sampled background pixels. The held-out score maps are evaluated only on the opposite official full asset and are calibrated with the same conservative background-threshold rule used for raw, residual, and TP-SSCS scores.

The feature-ensemble boundary uses the same LOSO split and empirical-Pfa rule. The raw/residual HGB receives raw-power and rank-30 residual local features. The TP-SSCS-feature HGB adds the compact TP-SSCS enhanced score, gate map, and cross-features. Both use 4096 sampled background pixels per training frame and all positive pixels from the training asset. This audit is intentionally reported even though compact TP-SSCS loses to the stronger raw/residual HGB, because it defines the supervised in-domain ceiling that the compact detector should not overclaim.

The positive-pixel label-efficiency audit uses the raw/residual HGB feature family but limits positive target supervision to `1`, `2`, `4`, `8`, `16`, `32`, `64`, `128`, `256`, `512`, or all source-domain positive target pixels. For each budget, three seeds sample positive target pixels on one official full asset and evaluate on every target-bearing frame of the opposite full asset, then swap train/test directions. Background samples are drawn only from the selected positive-pixel source frames, and all methods are thresholded by the same empirical-Pfa rule.

The label-cost Pareto audit reuses the frozen low-label HGB rows, reconstructs a frame-seed log-Pfa AUC for each positive-pixel budget, and pairs that AUC with the local CPU runtime profile. A point is Pareto-dominated when another point has no more positive target labels, no greater measured runtime, and no lower AUC, with at least one strict improvement. The audit is resource accounting over existing outputs, not another model-training run.

The target-free calibration audit estimates score thresholds only from the 23 target-free frames available in each official full asset. In same-asset mode, each target-bearing test asset uses thresholds estimated from its own target-free frames. In cross-asset mode, each target-bearing test asset uses thresholds estimated from the other full asset's target-free frames. The audit then reports both Pd deltas and measured empirical Pfa on target-bearing backgrounds.

The frame-level robustness audit reuses the frozen detector-candidate rows from the official full-asset evaluation. For each target-bearing frame and Pfa point, it computes TP-SSCS minus comparator Pd deltas against raw maps and `low_rank_residual_k30`, then reports win, tie, loss, nonnegative fraction, quantiles, and minimum deltas by asset and in the combined 210-frame population. It is a distributional support audit over the existing official result, not a new dataset.

The paired significance audit applies exact one-sided sign tests to those same paired frame-level deltas and then applies BH-FDR across all asset-level and combined comparator/Pfa tests. Ties are excluded from the sign-test denominator. The audit reports corrected q-values and matched sign effect sizes, so it complements but does not replace the paired bootstrap intervals.

The log-Pfa AUC audit reconstructs each target-bearing frame's Pd curve over the seven official Pfa points, integrates Pd over log10(Pfa), and normalizes by the checked log-Pfa span. A paired frame-bootstrap is then applied to TP-SSCS-minus-comparator AUC deltas. This summarizes the official operating surface without adding new operating points or extrapolating outside the checked grid.

The component-attribution audit reuses the frozen detector-candidate CSVs and compares four endpoints: raw power, rank-30 residual power, the trainable-gate score alone, and the selected finished detector. It reports operating-point deltas and log-Pfa AUC deltas by paired target-bearing frame. The audit is not a retraining run; it separates mechanism from policy selection and explicitly records the gate-only loose-Pfa boundary.

The runtime audit uses the same official full-asset frame shape and a deterministic target-bearing frame sample from both `simMed_test.mat` and `simWind_test.mat`. It times raw score construction, rank-30 residual formation, TP-SSCS forward/materialization, seven-Pfa finished-detector thresholding, raw/residual HGB feature construction, and HGB prediction on the local CPU. The reported numbers are used only for local complexity comparison and should not be generalized across hardware.

## Discussion Insert

The strengthened baseline audit reduces the risk that the main AISTAP-SIM result is an artifact of a weak or under-tuned comparator. The best swept comparator changes with Pfa and asset, including raw GOCA at the strictest combined point, low-rank GOCA/CA at intermediate points, and low-rank OS-CFAR at looser points. TP-SSCS remains ahead across the sweep, which supports the interpretation that the gate adds target-preserving information beyond residual cleaning, local background normalization, and the checked CFAR-parameter choices.

The learned-baseline audit addresses a different criticism: that TP-SSCS might only beat hand-designed CFAR variants. The LOSO logistic detector is still a bounded in-domain comparator rather than a large pretrained radar network, but it is supervised on official full-asset labels and tested on the opposite condition. TP-SSCS remains ahead with positive bootstrap support, which strengthens the AISTAP-SIM claim without changing the external boundary.

The stronger HGB audit narrows the claim. It shows that a sufficiently flexible supervised in-domain feature ensemble can exceed compact TP-SSCS. The TP-SSCS-feature HGB result recovers much of this gap and slightly improves the raw/residual HGB at most combined operating points, but it is not a strict all-Pfa win. The manuscript should therefore present compact TP-SSCS as an interpretable target-preserving detector policy and TP-SSCS features as useful supervised-detector inputs, not as a blanket winner over all learned detectors.

The positive-pixel budget audit gives the compact detector a more precise advantage: when only a handful of target instances are available, the zero-target-label TP-SSCS policy remains ahead of a supervised raw/residual HGB across the full low-false-alarm operating grid. This should be framed as a target-instance-scarcity result, not as a contradiction of the full-label HGB boundary.

The label-cost Pareto audit makes that advantage more concrete. It shows that the compact policy is not merely winning selected low-label operating points; it remains a lower-label, lower-runtime, higher-AUC point through the checked `16`-positive-pixel HGB regime. The same audit also marks the transition: at `64` positive target pixels, the HGB becomes the higher-AUC choice despite its greater label and runtime cost.

The target-free calibration audit preempts a different reviewer criticism. It shows that TP-SSCS does not only win because thresholds were estimated from target-bearing frame backgrounds; the ordering remains favorable when thresholds are estimated from target-free support. At the same time, empirical Pfa drift under fixed threshold transfer confirms that the paper should not claim a deployment-ready fixed-threshold calibrator.

The frame-level robustness audit addresses whether the official mean Pd gain is driven by only a few target-bearing frames. Against the rank-matched low-rank residual, the answer is no under the checked frame-Pfa grid: every item-Pfa pair is nonnegative, though many loose-Pfa comparisons are ties. Against raw maps, the answer is broader but not absolute: most frames favor TP-SSCS, but raw-favorable cases remain and should be reported rather than hidden.

The paired significance audit turns that distributional result into formal nonparametric support. The corrected p-value and sign-effect results are strong enough for manuscript reporting, but the interpretation should stay at the paired-frame level because adjacent pixels within a frame are not independent trials and ties are excluded by the sign test.

The log-Pfa AUC audit addresses a related reviewer concern: whether the result is a collection of isolated operating-point wins. Integrating across the full checked Pfa grid keeps the comparison tied to the same empirical-threshold protocol while showing that the advantage persists across the operating surface. The limitation is explicit: no claim is made for false-alarm rates outside the evaluated grid.

The component-attribution audit clarifies what the selected detector is doing. The residual branch supplies broad structured-clutter suppression, and the zero-false gate branch adds target support at the strictest false-alarm point. Gate-only is useful as a learned-score endpoint, but its loose-Pfa advantage means it should be treated as an alternative relaxed endpoint, not as evidence that the selected conservative policy maximizes Pd everywhere.

The runtime audit adds a practical boundary to the HGB comparison. The supervised HGB is a stronger in-domain detector when enough labels are available, but its checked inference stack is heavier on the local CPU because it requires residual formation, feature construction, and ensemble prediction. This supports positioning compact TP-SSCS as a lower-cost target-preserving policy under the measured implementation, while hardware-independent speed claims remain out of scope.

The seed-sensitivity result strengthens reproducibility but does not remove all training uncertainty. It shows that nearby trainable-gate initializations preserve the official full-asset win pattern under the checked architecture and schedule. It should not be phrased as universal seed invariance. Together, the seed and stronger-baseline audits move the AISTAP-SIM evidence closer to a top-tier experimental standard while preserving the existing external boundary: IPIX supports validation-selected fusion, and SSDD supports supervised adaptation rather than unmodified zero-shot transfer.
